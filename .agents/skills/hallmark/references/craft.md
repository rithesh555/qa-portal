# Craft

The one file every page build reads. Type, colour, layout, hero, motion, copy, section openings, demo slots and finish, as numbers wherever a number exists. The theme file supplies the values; this file supplies the rules the values sit in. Gate numbers in parentheses point at `gates.md`.

## 1. Type

Type carries the page. At most two families, a display face and a body face, which may be one family. A third is one **outlier** register (a wordmark, a headline figure, code) and it carries exactly one role (mono for code and for measured figures counts as one); a second role makes it a third family in running use (37, F13). Same family at different weights is one family.

- **Extremes read as chosen.** Three weights at most across the page, display and body together (F19), picked as extremes: 300 beside 800, or 400 beside 700.
- **A ratio, not increments.** 1.25 by default (1.333 or 1.5 for more drama), built from a 16px body. At most five sizes in running use, plus a wordmark size if the theme names one; hierarchy past that comes from weight and colour.
- **Display:** tracking -0.02 to -0.04em on a regular-width face, 0 to -0.015em on a condensed or very heavy one (they are drawn tight already; two letters touching is a fault, not a style), line-height 1.0 to 1.15 (tighter as it grows; 1.2 reads as inherited; a mixed-case poster head may close to 0.92). Uppercase heads that can wrap never go under 1.0 (55). A head is one weight, one style and one colour throughout; one word set apart by italic, colour, weight or a highlight is the accented word, the field's most-named tell after the eyebrow (38). Stress comes from a line break or a second line at a second size. Default display size `clamp(2.75rem, 5vw + 1rem, 5.25rem)`; cap 5.5rem, 6rem on display-heavy themes, 7rem only for a single word; a poster-scale theme or shape (Manifesto, Marquee Hero, a Poster hero; Broadside fits its lines to the measure by its own rule, and an HP2 overflow head is sized to bleed) sizes with the screen up to the container's cap, `clamp(4rem, 10vw, 9rem)`, so it reaches full size at 1440 and holds there at 1920 where the container holds (Floor 14), while the fold still holds the headline, one sentence and the action.
- **Body:** 16px minimum (14px never for body), line-height 1.5 to 1.65, measure 45 to 75ch (`max-width: 65ch`) (25). Never all-caps body, never justified without hyphenation, never tracking above 0.05em on body.
- **Labels, captions, table heads, meta, tags, chips, nav links:** the body face, a step smaller, sentence case (or lowercase on a theme whose voice is lowercase), untracked (67). Never uppercase, never small caps, never letter-spaced. Mono only when the content is code, a figure or a measurement, or when mono is the theme's material (64). Capitals belong to a wordmark and to a theme whose display voice is uppercase; there a control may follow the display's case at 0.04em of tracking or less.
- **Headline sizing by copy length.** Count the rendered `h1`: 20 characters or fewer, full display (a single word may grow to 7rem); 21 to 50, display; 51 to 90, one step down (`--text-display-s`) or split at the sense break with `<br>`; over 90, rewrite. Self-written headlines aim for seven words and 50 characters.
- **Heading measure rides on the element**, in `ch`: the scaffold sets `h1` 20ch, `h2` 30ch, `h3` 40ch; a head at display-s takes about 24ch. Never a `max-width` on the heading group's wrapper.
- **Required:** `font-display: swap`; a fallback stack of the same build (a grotesk falls back to `system-ui`, a serif to Georgia), with `size-adjust` and the overrides only where the face's metrics are published, never guessed; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on any figures that compare; `text-wrap: balance` on heads, `pretty` on ledes and body; curly quotes, a true ellipsis, a non-breaking space inside unit pairs (`10 MB`, `⌘ K`) in a proportional face (in mono the unit sits outside the mono span or takes a plain space, since a mono space is a full cell). No `--` and no em or en dash anywhere in rendered copy; a comma, a colon or a full stop does the work.
- **Wordmark.** May take a different face from the body; on tone-rich themes it should. Collapse to one family only when the body voice is the brand (a letter, a manifesto, a long document).
- **Load only the weights you use.** Never synthesise bold or italic.

Font sourcing for a custom run is in `fonts.md`; a catalog theme has already named its faces.

## 2. Colour

- **OKLCH everywhere.** Perceptual lightness is what makes contrast predictable.
- **Four layers.** Paper (light: L 92 to 98, chroma 0.005 to 0.015 toward the anchor hue; dark: L 12 to 18, 0.008 to 0.015). Ink (light paper: L 16 to 24; dark paper: L 88 to 96; tinted 0.005 to 0.014). Five to nine neutrals between them, each tinted 0.005 to 0.018 (22). One accent at chroma 0.12 to 0.22 with a paired `--color-accent-ink` (41). A focus colour: the accent's hue, chroma 0.18 to 0.22, L within 5% of the accent.
- **Never a pure absolute** as a base surface or as an improvised value (7). Tint the greys toward the anchor: a warm accent wants warm neutrals.
- **The accent is a signal, not a fill.** Under 5% of any viewport under the default Restrained posture (23): an active nav item, a focus ring, a link underline on hover, the primary control, the one series a chart argues from. If you want more, that is the reflex.
- **Postures.** Restrained (above). **Committed:** one colour carries 30 to 60% of the surface through `--color-field` or the paper family; the accent proper stays a signal. **Full palette:** three or four named roles (field, signal, seal, ink), each with a job and a footprint. **Drenched:** the surface is the colour; ink and rules derive from it. A posture is declared in the stamp before code; a declared surface colour is not accent footprint.
- **Contrast.** Body 4.5:1 (target 7:1) or APCA Lc 60; large text, icons, focus rings and component boundaries 3:1 or Lc 45 (40). Where you cannot render, keep the two lightnesses 15% or more apart. Every rule that paints a dark surface swaps its text colour in the same rule (41). Grey text never sits on a coloured surface: tint it from the hue or use paper.
- **Bans:** gradients of two or three stops only, and never purple-blue, purple-pink, cyan-on-dark (2a); no glow shadows on dark, no chromatic halos or spotlights (29); no alpha as a colour's definition (alpha modifies overlays and shadows); no red-green as the only signal.
- **Dark mode** is opt-in and derived, never inverted: paper 12 to 18, ink 92 to 96, elevation lighter by about 3% L per level, accent chroma down 0.02 to 0.04 with L up 5 to 10, hue fixed. `dark-mode.md` has the per-token table when both modes are asked for.

## 3. Layout and space

- **A primary axis.** Left-biased, right-biased, top-heavy or bottom-weighted. Centre-biased is a default, not a choice; at most two centred elements in a hero, one breaking alignment (6b).
- **Spacing is a scale.** 4pt base, ten steps, named by size: `--space-3xs` 2px · `2xs` 4 · `xs` 8 · `sm` 12 · `md` 16 · `lg` 24 · `xl` 40 · `2xl` 64 · `3xl` 96 · `4xl` 144. Raw lengths where a token exists are a Floor failure (24a); at most three optical nudges off the scale (24b). `gap` for siblings, `margin` only to break the flow.
- **Between sections, three tokens** (59): `--section-gap-s` 120px, `--section-gap` 144px and `--section-gap-l` 200px at desktop, stepping down together at 60rem and 40rem; mix them so the rhythm varies, never one raw length. A change of paper may take the small step. Inside a section, a heading's own line sits 8 to 16px under it, its content 32 to 48px, and grid items 24 to 40px apart. At least twice as much space above any heading as below it. Four to eight sections a page, footer included, usually 3,000 to 6,000px tall at 1440; a thin brief makes a shorter page, never a padded one.
- **The container is capped and centred:** `max-width` 1360 to 1520px, gutters 64 to 96px at 1440 and 200px or more at 1920 (44d). Grids hold their column count above 1440; `auto-fit` that grows a fourth column changes the composition. Visuals sized in `vw` carry a `max-width`.
- **Page invariants** (F15) are in SKILL.md Step 6; with them, one secondary button style, and one horizontal padding applied by the same two-element pattern in every section (outer element: background and vertical padding; inner: `.container`) so edges line up while scrolling. The same kind of thing always gets the same font treatment. The hero's gap is 44c's. Never a centred layout directly under a left-aligned one unless a full-width band, a paper change or a rule separates them; centre only heroes, CTAs and sections with symmetric content beneath.
- **Heading groups.** A headline and at most one line beneath. Constrain each text element with its own `max-width` in `ch`, never the wrapper. A grid or list that fills its section takes the section's edges: never a narrower `max-width` with auto margins on it; a nested width belongs only to a self-contained unit (a form, a pricing set, a centred figure). If the user asked for a centred subhead over 120 characters, offer a shorter line first.
- **Coherence over side.** A head may be left, centred, hanging, bottom-anchored or overlapping; what reads as a mistake is a narrow auto-centred head floating over full-width left-flush content.
- **Grids.** CSS Grid for the page, Flexbox for component internals. Break the equal-columns reflex: `1.2fr 1fr 0.8fr`, a 12-column grid with unequal spans, a 4-up with a 2-span lead (3). Named areas for complex layouts. Flex children that must shrink get `min-width: 0`; icons, logos and images get `flex-shrink: 0`. A grid has no empty cell and no orphan: a last row of one under a fuller row, or six logos as five and one, is a count to change (F20).
- **Asymmetry that reads as chosen:** a wide margin column carrying captions and footnotes beside the body (never a label beside a heading, which is an eyebrow); a heading that hangs into the margin; offset column widths; one element that crosses a column boundary; generous top and tight bottom. Rows that pair text with a visual keep the visual on one side and vary by weight (one lead row with the larger visual); a mirror may happen once, never a zigzag down the page.
- **The surface ladder.** Whitespace, then a hairline rule (1px `--color-rule`, inset or full-bleed), then a tinted well (`--color-paper-2` or `-3`, no border), then a card (a border or a shadow, never a hairline under a wide soft shadow, F24; reserved for content that is independently interactive or of a different kind). Never white cards on a tinted page; never a card in a card, and never a bordered card or label box inside a well: a well holds rows, figures or a drawn object (4); never jump to the top rung (9, F7). Sibling items in a shared context (stats, KPIs) separate with a top border or a vertical hairline: the first item has no leading padding, the last no trailing, and the rule pattern is reset at every breakpoint where the column count changes.
- **Depth is weight and scale**, not shadow. One shadow if any: whisper `0 1px 2px oklch(20% 0.01 <hue> / 0.05)` or hairline `0 0 0 1px oklch(30% 0.01 <hue> / 0.06)`. Never a border and a wide soft shadow on the same box; commit to one. Elevated surfaces are never darker than their canvas. Eight named z levels: base 1, raised 10, dropdown 100, sticky 200, sticky-nav 300, modal 400, toast 500, tooltip 600; never a freestyle z (56).
- **Page-edge clipping.** `html, body { overflow-x: clip }` on every page (34); `clip` keeps `sticky` and `fixed` working where `hidden` breaks them. The section that holds a deliberately overflowing element keeps `overflow: visible`.
- **Radii.** One radius per level; nested radii are concentric: `inner = outer - padding`, clamped at 2px. Image and screenshot radii scale with the viewport: `min(1vw, var(--radius-card))`.
- **Icons.** One library per project, one stroke width and one style (outline or filled) per page; sizes 16, 20, 24, 32 at the set's natural grid (a 24 viewBox at 24px, never scaled to 19); `currentColor`; beside multi-line text align to the first line; never inside a coloured coin or tile (3, F5); inline with small text use the 16px size. Buttons and badges with an icon take asymmetric padding: the icon side's padding equals the vertical padding.
- **When flat, before shipping:** add one break-out element, unbalance a column, move the primary CTA off centre, replace a card with air, or make one section's padding uneven.

## 4. The hero

The highest-stakes surface on the page. Pick a posture at Step 2, stamp it, and vary it run to run.

| Posture | Character | Height at desktop | Padding | Pairs with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Settled** (default) | balanced fold, sits into the page | 70 to 90% of the viewport | block-end 1.3 times block-start | any hero archetype |
| **Banner** | compressed opener, the page starts fast | 40 to 55dvh, cap `min(52dvh, 34rem)` | 1.3 times, edge-led | reference, docs, utility |
| **Poster** | full-bleed canvas, the fold is the artwork | 92 to 100dvh (the only legal 100dvh) | waived when content is corner-anchored | atmospheric, playful, Manifesto |
| **Ledge** | tall void above, content rests at the base | 70 to 88dvh | block-start 2 times block-end, `align-content: end`; at 1920 x 1080 the head grows to its cap before the void passes 45% of the screen | sport, brutal, photographic |
| **Corridor** | tall centred reading column, 44ch or less | 66 to 85dvh | 1.3 times | editorial, atelier |
| **Stage** | text column beside a full-height visual that may bleed | 70 to 92dvh | 1.3 times on the text column | modern-minimal, playful product |

Every posture, every hero:

- **Fits the fold at 1280 x 800** (44b): headline, one sentence, primary action and the visual's focal point all visible without scrolling. Culprits when it does not: an oversized display clamp, line-height near 1.2, a three-line lede, padding bloat. Right-size, never cramp.
- **Is exactly one screen at 1440 x 900 and 1920 x 1080** (44c): the next section starts between y 760 and y 900 at 1440. Too short with dead space under it is the same fault as spilling into a second screen. Fill with the visual or the headline, never with a third paragraph.
- **The visual sits beside the text**: vertical centres within 60px, 48 to 120px between the text block's edge and the visual, never touching the viewport edge unless a deliberate full-bleed. The corner-pinned device beside a left-hugging text block is the most recognisable machine-made hero there is.
- **Three things in the text**: headline, one sentence, actions (58). No label above, no small print under the buttons, no badge row, no version pill (66). A caption inside the visual belongs to the visual, twelve words at most.
- **Mobile** under 60rem collapses every posture to content height (Poster may stay full-bleed).
- **Entrance:** one orchestrated settle of 0.4 to 0.8s including its stagger, from an already-visible default; a fade with an 8px rise is one motion. Never a fade plus a sweep plus a scale on one element; never bounce; never scroll-fade on every section; never a starfield, a mouse-parallax or an auto-rotating carousel.
- **One polish pattern at most** (a vertical rail title, a marquee overflow, a decorative numeral that means something). Two is noise.

**Enrichment** is an option, never a default. The hierarchy: typography only → pure CSS art → hand-built SVG → generated still → customised library asset → Lottie, last. The image-need check in `hero-enrichment.md` decides whether the brief wants imagery at all; write the scene sentence first (a real person, a real time and place, with the product) because it fixes the surface's lightness and light temperature. Before an enrichment ships, all of these are yes: it communicates something the type cannot; under 2 MB; has a reduced-motion fallback; if video, muted, looped, `playsinline`, with a poster, preloaded when it is the LCP; built with intent, not picked from a library; keeps the accent under its footprint; survives deletion (the hero still works without it); matches the page's tone. Any no, ship the typographic hero.

## 5. Motion

- **Who decides.** The theme's Motion section; a shape's reveal runs only where the theme allows one, and the genre's default holds only where the theme is silent.
- **End states.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every reveal shows its end state at once, and the render tier shoots that way, so a rule that draws in on scroll must already be drawn there.
- **Only `transform` and `opacity`** (10). Blur, `clip-path`, mask and shadow may join when they stay smooth on the element alone.
- **Three easings, tokenised:** `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for things arriving, `--ease-in: cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.84, 0)` for things leaving, `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` for toggles. The browser's `ease` and `linear` (outside progress) read as uncrafted; overshoot on UI state is dated (12).
- **Three durations:** `--dur-micro` 120ms (press, tick, colour), `--dur-short` 220ms (hover, tooltip, menu), `--dur-long` 420ms (modal, drawer, accordion, page reveal). Exits run 60 to 75% of the enter. Stagger 30 to 50ms between rows, 60 to 100ms between blocks, 500ms of stagger at most, one-shot, never re-fired on scroll.
- **One reveal per page**, staggered by a `--i` custom property, the whole settle 0.4 to 0.8s, and the content is visible without it (62): scope the hidden state to a `.js` class the script adds, or animate from visible with `@starting-style`. Under reduced motion the scaffold's base ends every transition and animation at once; what should still run (a loader, a 150ms fade) takes `.keep-motion`.
- **Scroll:** IntersectionObserver for reveals and for any state that flips at a threshold; a passive scroll listener, throttled to one read per frame, only where direction matters (a bar that hides on the way down); reveal once; scroll-scrubbed motion exists only as the named patterns in `scroll-choreography.md`, one per page (F8).
- **State:** hover is one signal (11) and raises contrast against the rest state: a colour step, or a `translateY(-1px)` lift at micro on a control that keeps its colour, active back to 0; menus `--ease-out` open and `--ease-in` close with the Popover API; modals scale 0.96 to 1 with a backdrop fade over `--dur-long` using `<dialog>` and `inert`; accordions animate `grid-template-rows: 0fr` to `1fr`; tooltips delay 800 to 1000ms on hover and 0ms on focus (17); focus rings never animate (15).
- **Never:** parallax, custom cursors, cursor followers, a marquee that will not pause on hover and focus (18), `will-change` on a whole class, an image that scales or rotates on hover (11), a pulsing dot (a live dot holds still, or blinks once when its value updates), a caret that blinks past five beats, and infinite loops, except a loader, one E6 loop (4s or less a cycle) and one ambient loop the theme declares as its material (8s or slower); every loop stops under reduced motion and while off-screen, and one that runs past 5 seconds carries a visible pause control (WCAG 2.2.2).

Full recipes (buttons, inputs, validation, toasts, modals, menus, drag, command palette, optimistic updates) are in `microinteractions.md`; load it when the page has stateful UI.

## 6. Copy

Words are design material. Specific verbs (`Save changes`, never `Submit`); labels that describe (`Email address`); link text that stands alone; errors that name what broke, why, and what to do; active voice; one word for one action across the page.

- **Budget** (58): hero subhead one sentence of twelve words or fewer; headlines seven words or fewer, section heads three to five; section intros one line; paragraphs two short sentences; lists stop at four items of a three-word label and a ten-word line (five when they are demands or steps that cannot merge; past that it is a table); tables at five rows; a set the user gave keeps its true count (six destinations are six rows); 450 visible words on a page at most, and fewer is fine: a thin brief makes a short page, never a padded one. Read only the headings and the hero: if they tell the story, the rest is the right length.
- **Openers.** Name the place, the date, the vertical, the number the user gave, the input the product reads. Refuse the verb-less claim. Never *Built for the modern team*, *Unleash*, *Where A meets B*, *Empower*, *Reimagine*, *Supercharge*, *seamless*, *robust*, *effortless*, *next-generation*, *world-class*, *In today's landscape* (19). The rebuttal cadence (*Not a feature. A platform.*) is fine once and a tic three times.
- **Buttons:** the verb of the action. `Continue` only as the secondary step of a flow. **Errors:** what happened (past tense), why if known, what to do (imperative); no *Oops*, no exclamation marks, no humour in a frustration path. **Empty states:** one line naming what is empty, one on why it matters, one action. **Loading:** a spinner appears after 150 to 300ms, stays at least 300ms and sits beside the control's label, never in its place; *Loading…* past 2s; honest progress past 10s.
- **Never** placeholder people (Jane Doe), startup clichés (Acme, Nexus), *Click here*, *Enter your email below*, *Quietly trusted by*, a version string in marketing copy, or a number the user did not give (46a).
- **Sample data** inside a code surface or a slot's sketch uses the reserved `example.com`, the field names the brief gives and values that read as samples (`Example Domain`, a word count that matches the text shown); never a figure a visitor could take as a claim.
- **Punctuation is typeset:** curly quotes, `…`, a proper apostrophe, no `--`, no em or en dash (a comma, a colon, a full stop or parentheses instead), non-breaking spaces before units. Periods on every standalone sentence, none on list items, consistently.

## 7. Opening a section

The reflex is a small tracked-caps label above the heading, and the reflex is the tell (54). The question is *what does the reader not yet know?* If nothing, open with nothing: a generous margin and a heading that starts flush is the most under-used opening there is. If something, it rides on a fact, a destination or an identifier, never on a name for what comes next. Twelve ways in:

1. **Nothing.** Air above, heading flush. Never goes stale.
2. **The heading announces itself.** Move the subject into its first two words. Usually the best move; the label existed because the heading was vague.
3. **A lead-in inside the paragraph**, in a heavier weight, never on its own line.
4. **A drop cap.** One per page, first paragraph only.
5. **A rule that does more than rule**: hairline, fleuron, double rule, colour edge, or pure gap; one divider language down the page, varied in weight and placement from seam to seam (9).
6. **A numeral at graphic scale**, one per page: a figure the page argues from at `clamp(6rem, 16vw, 14rem)`, a decorative one behind a head to 18rem, only when the number is real (an issue, a year, a version, a chapter).
7. **A real `<ol>`** with `counter()` when the sequence is genuine.
8. **A caption below** the content instead of above it: a plate in a catalogue, not a slide title.
9. **Marginalia** beside the body in the outer margin.
10. **A change of paper.** The band's surface changes; that edge is the break. No words.
11. **A running head or folio**: continuous chrome at the page edge (a sticky folio, a rail), legal because it persists rather than announces.
12. **Persistence**: the heading stays in view while its content scrolls beneath.

Two moves can share a section; three is noise. Pick one opening and repeat it, or two and alternate; rotate against the log so one opening does not become your signature. Still legal, because each names something present: a `<figcaption>`, a `<th>`, a `<label>`, a `<legend>`, a nav or tab label, a byline or a `<time>` dateline, a unit after its figure, a code block's real filename, a live `role="status"` chip, one linked announcement that goes somewhere real, a plan name (which usually wants to be the heading).

## 8. Demo slots

A landing page is a demo. The first screen shows the thing working; later screens show it from other angles. Prose is what a page falls back on when it has nothing to show.

A slot is a container carrying `data-slot="<name>"` (`hero`, `demo-2`, `demo-3`, `photo-1`) and a `data-alt` describing what will be in it, at a real size (an `aspect-ratio` or a fixed height, never the image's own), holding a designed placeholder that the user's image replaces without touching the layout. The CSS ships in the scaffold's `base.css` and the script in every page that has a slot; in a framework the same contract is a `<Slot name="hero">` component.

```css
[data-slot] { position: relative; overflow: hidden; }
[data-slot] > img { position: absolute; inset: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; z-index: 1; }
[data-slot]:has(> img) > :not(img):not([data-keep]) { display: none !important; }
```

A child that must stay once the image lands (a plate number, a credit) carries `data-keep` and sits above the image (`z-index: 2`).

```js
window.hallmarkSlots = (m) => { for (const [k, v] of Object.entries(m)) { const s = document.querySelector(`[data-slot="${k}"]`); if (!s) continue; const im = new Image(); im.src = typeof v === 'string' ? v : v.src; im.alt = (typeof v === 'object' && v.alt) || s.dataset.alt || ''; s.prepend(im); } };
```

**The placeholder is designed, not grey.** A gradient of two or three OKLCH stops from the theme's paper and accent with a slow hue path (a mesh of two radial gradients is the richer version on a light theme, washing from an edge; a dark theme lifts a corner by a lightness step, a radial only where the genre allows blooms), optionally a sparse drawn interface of five shapes or fewer in the theme's material, one corner label in the theme's caption style saying what goes there (`your screenshot`, `product photo`), inside a frame that suits the theme (a hairline panel with a slight tilt, a device silhouette, a plain rounded panel, a kiss-cut print, an enamel plate). Never traffic lights, a URL pill or a notch (47a); never invented figures presented as real (47c); never the word *placeholder* at display size.

Four recipes, one per material family:

- **Paper** (editorial, print): `linear-gradient(160deg, var(--color-paper-2), var(--color-paper-3))` with a `repeating-linear-gradient` of 1px rules at 4% ink every 32px; a 1px `--color-rule` border; label bottom-left in the caption style.
- **Screen** (modern-minimal, product): a 16:10 panel over `--color-paper-2` (on a Restrained theme whose accent is a signal only, `--color-paper-3`; elsewhere `radial-gradient(at 20% 0%, <accent at 18% chroma-cut>, transparent 60%)` over it), holding the product's own structure in three to five shapes drawn from what the brief says it does (event rows for a webhook tool, dose rows and a tick for a care app, a waveform for audio), the label in one corner; never an empty head row over an empty card outline, and never a top bar with pills or dots, which reads as a browser (47a).
- **Dark** (atmospheric, night): `linear-gradient(170deg, var(--color-paper-2), var(--color-paper-3))`, a 1px inset ring of ink at 8% alpha; label bottom-right in `--color-muted`. Only a theme whose genre allows blooms adds one radial in the accent at 20% alpha from a corner; a theme that bans radial light keeps the flat step.
- **Print object** (playful, sticker): the accent field with a 2px ink border, a second colour block offset 8px behind it (zero blur, dropped where the theme forbids hard offsets), a dashed kiss-cut line inset 6px; label as a small rotated tag in the corner.

**Text slots.** A missing fact (a venue, a date, an act, a price) is a text slot, never an invented value: `<span class="fill" data-fill="venue">Venue to be announced</span>`, set in the body face in `--color-muted` on a faint tint of it (`background: color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-muted) 14%, transparent)`, 2px radius, no underline) so it reads as a blank to fill, never as a link, at most three on a page, each listed in the handoff with what belongs there. Text slots do not count toward 68. A typeface the page is about but the user has not supplied is a font slot: the display token names it first, a free face of the same build stands in and says so in one caption, and the handoff asks for the files. A section the user named but gave nothing for is its heading, one line on what will go there and one text slot, or it merges into a neighbour; never a block of pending rows.

Where slots go: the hero visual on any product page, beside the text; a demo strip of two or three slots with one label of three words or fewer each, never three equal columns: one leads at about twice the width (this replaces the three-card feature grid); photo slots on business pages. Two to four slots carry a product page; more only when the user has handed over the images. A set of things the page does not have pictures of (a team, a range, a list of venues) is set in type, names and facts in a list or a table, with one slot at most: four empty frames in a row is a wireframe, not a page (68). The signature never lives inside a slot, since the user's image replaces everything in it. Given images go straight in and are named in the Picks block; "leave slots" builds the placeholders and the handoff lists every slot, its size and what belongs there; silence builds from what is real, with a slot only where a picture is the point.

## 9. Finish

The last 2% that separates set from generated. sloplint's F-checks report most of it; answer each one, never ship it unread.

- **Controls.** One filled primary per page, dialogs counted as their own page (F4); secondaries outlined or typographic and never higher contrast than the primary; navbar actions quieter than the hero's action. Marketing controls 44 to 52px tall; app UI 32 to 40px with a 44px hit target via an inset pseudo-element and 48px on coarse pointers; at most two button sizes, at least 6px apart; inputs match the height of their adjacent button (39). Icon-bearing controls take asymmetric padding. Any non-primary form action (change photo, resend code) takes the smaller size and a secondary style. Delete uses a secondary style unless deleting is the page's one action.
- **Links that go nowhere yet.** An action whose URL the brief does not give points at the section of this page that does the most of it (`#lookbook`), and the handoff names the URL it needs; never an invented route.
- **Forms that post nowhere yet.** A form whose endpoint the brief does not give carries an empty `data-endpoint` and never prints a success state: no "Sent", "Reserved" or "You're on the list" for a request that did not happen (70); it says plainly that nothing was sent. The handoff names the endpoint it needs.
- **Inputs.** Constant border width across states; the focus ring is an `outline` with an offset (inset on inputs), never a border change; disabled by three channels together, `opacity: 0.55`, `cursor: not-allowed` and the native `disabled` attribute (39a); reserve `min-height: 1lh` under the field for helper text (F14); 16px on mobile; a `name`, `autocomplete`, the matching `type` and `inputmode` on every field, with a `<label>` or `aria-label`; paste never blocked; submit enabled until the request starts, and a failed submit focuses the first invalid field; an explicit `type` on every `<button>`; a custom chevron on selects; native checkbox, radio and toggle styled by state in CSS, never toggled by script.
- **Type finish.** Hanging punctuation on pull quotes (`::before` with the opening quote translated -100%). Tabular numerals on anything that columns (F6). Balanced heads, pretty body (F3). A testimonial exists only when the user supplied it (46a); its card bottom-aligns the attribution, and the avatar is the real person or nothing.
- **Pricing.** Emphasise a tier with its button and a word, never a different card colour; align buttons across tiers by pushing them to the bottom; a taller featured tier is a grid sibling on explicit rows (`grid-template-rows: <gap> 1fr <gap>`, the featured card spanning all rows), never negative margins.
- **Tables.** Sentence-case heads that never wrap, horizontal rules only, no outer border, no card around a table, full width. On a phone the column that names the row and the one that acts on it stay in view (F25): restack each row as a labelled block, or pin the first column and let the rest scroll. A bare scrolling wrapper hides the amount and the action.
- **Responsive finish.** Body-adjacent UI (meta, nav links, controls, checkboxes) renders larger on mobile and steps down at 60rem; display heads are exempt; body never drops below 16px on a phone. Every desktop sidebar or filter column collapses into a menu or disclosure. Container queries for widgets whose layout depends on their box, placed on the closest wrapper.
- **Browser surfaces** (F16). The scaffold's `base.css` themes the selection, the caret, the scrollbar, the focus ring and the underline; keep them, and set numerals tabular wherever they compare. The parts you did not draw still carry the design.
- **Nav.** One line, 56 to 80px tall; a sticky one taller than the scaffold's `scroll-padding-top` raises it so anchors and focus land clear. Text-only links in the top bar, one weight across default, hover and active (state is a colour or a soft background, never a primary fill); a tab row scrolls sideways rather than wrapping; under 60rem, links that no longer fit go behind a menu, while two or three short ones that fit stay in the bar; the logo is a link home with an `aria-label`.
- **The document.** `lang` on `<html>`, a `<title>` in the page's own words, a one-sentence meta description (F23); heading levels step one at a time and CSS sets their size (F21); every `<img>` carries `width`, `height` and a file that exists (76).
- **Prose pages** (articles, docs): line-height 1.65 to 1.75, one family for the title and its subheads, `text-wrap: pretty` on the title, a measure of 60 to 75ch (25).
- **Stat rows and dashboards.** A stat's title never wraps, no icon sits in a stat, siblings part with a top or side hairline, and a widget's layout answers its container (`@container`), not the viewport.
- **Definition lists.** Features and metadata are a `<dl>`: `<dt>` one weight heavier and higher contrast, `<dd>` regular and quieter, so a link inside the `<dd>` still reads.
- **Compact forms** (sign-in, one field): 20rem wide, never an input and a button sharing one border.
- **Motion finish.** Enters `--ease-out`, exits `--ease-in` (the pair most often reversed); exits at 60 to 75%.
- **Assets.** One icon set; images take an inset outline (`outline: 1px solid <ink at 5 to 10% alpha>; outline-offset: -1px`) instead of a border; `alt=""` when the subject is named beside the image; logos 20 to 28px tall in a footer, footer links at the body weight, footer icons never lighter than `--color-muted`; logo rows split evenly (3 + 3, never 5 + 1) and take the hero's alignment when they sit under it.
